1.0.0 2026-08-24
  • The loop is closed: specification in, verified schematic out. In the acceptance run, one sentence went into the panel and a strategist chose the two-stage topology for the gain requirement, generated a design from the targets, measured it, iterated to feasibility in six simulations, then verified it across all eleven PVT conditions.
  • Verified means verified: the shipped design holds every target at every corner. Worst gain 69.4 dB at low supply and hot, worst bandwidth 3.77 MHz at the slow corner, worst phase margin 63.3 degrees, worst power 67.2 microwatts, each named with the condition that caused it.
  • The whole flow is pinned by an end-to-end test that replays it with real tools, and the winning schematic is reproducible: rerunning its exact parameters lands on its exact numbers.
  • Deployment groundwork ships with it: a Dockerfile, continuous integration that runs the suite against a real ngspice on every push, and DEPLOY.md with the honest warnings about authentication and API credits.
  • What verified does not yet mean: layout, parasitic extraction, or silicon. Those are the next chapters of the plan, not fine print.
0.9.0 2026-08-24
  • PVT corners. A Robustness panel on the SKY130 circuits reruns the design across eleven conditions: five process corners, the supply and temperature extremes, and the two classic cross corners. The table names the worst case per metric and the condition that caused it. Measured on the OTA: unity-gain bandwidth falls from 14.3 MHz nominal to 11.4 MHz at slow, hot and starved.
  • Monte Carlo mismatch: twenty samples from the PDK's statistical models at the typical corner, each under its own seed, reported as mean, sigma, minimum and maximum. The PDK's bare mc section ships without device models, so mismatch at the typical corner is what Monte Carlo means here, and the manual says so.
  • The machinery is one hook: robustness runs edit the finished netlist text, the corner in the .lib line, the supply, the temperature card. No circuit builder learned anything.
  • The strategist gained run_corners and now verifies a finished design across PVT, reporting the worst case with its corner named.
0.8.0 2026-08-24
  • A second amplifier topology: the five-transistor OTA on SKY130, measured open loop through the same DC servo as the two-stage. One stage means no compensation, phase margin near 90 degrees, and better bandwidth per microwatt, at the price of gain: 37 dB at the base sizing against the two-stage's 71.5 dB.
  • Topology selection is now a real choice the strategist makes and explains: high gain points at the two-stage, modest gain with speed or power pressure points at the OTA. Both topologies seed from targets and both can be iterated.
  • The measured trade is pinned by a test: the OTA carries more than ten degrees more margin, the two-stage more than twenty decibels more gain.
  • View netlist, under the run button: the exact SPICE deck your values produce, updating live as you edit. Nothing on this site is hidden behind the schematic; the deck is the ground truth and now you can read it.
0.7.1 2026-08-24
  • First contact with the real OpenAI API, and the fixes it demanded: current models take max_completion_tokens, and function tools on the chat endpoint need reasoning effort disabled. The default model is now gpt-5.6-terra, overridable through FARADAEM_OPENAI_MODEL.
  • Rate limits and transient provider errors are retried with backoff, honouring Retry-After.
  • Strategist tools accept partial parameters merged over the circuit's defaults, so a model can pass just the load capacitance, and the tool schemas now carry the real circuit ids and target keys so first calls land.
  • A design card's parameters now include the fixed conditions, not only the tuned values. Applying a card reproduces the card's measurement exactly; before this, the load could silently revert to the form's value.
  • Verified live: a plain-English request became a measured op-amp meeting all four targets, 72.1 dB, 4.73 MHz, 65.9 degrees, 65.9 microwatts, designed by GPT driving the seed, simulate and iterate tools.
0.7.0 2026-08-24
  • The LLM layer. An Ask for a design panel takes a plain-language request, and a strategist drives the real tools: it reads the catalogue, sets targets, seeds, simulates, and iterates. Its words render as words; every number renders as a card taken from a tool result, with a button that loads the design into the form and reruns it.
  • The strategist never produces a circuit value, and when it believes a target should change, it asks instead of deciding. Sessions can be continued, stopped, and started over.
  • Two providers over the standard library alone: the Anthropic and OpenAI APIs through urllib, selected by whichever keys exist, with a picker when both do. Keys are read from the user environment, including keys set with setx after the server started. No new dependencies.
  • The whole layer is tested without a network against a scripted model, including tool failures, the turn limit, pausing on questions, and a full session over HTTP.
  • The four-way comparison harness from the research plan: human design, optimizer only, LLM only, and LLM plus optimizer, per provider, measured on the same spec by the same simulator. First offline result: the optimizer already edges out the hand design, 131 against 156 microwatts at the same spec.

0.6.1 2026-08-24
  • Spec-first generation on the op-amp: enter only the targets, press one button, and a design is created, measured, iterated if it falls short, and confirmed with a final simulation. No starting sizes needed.
  • The starting design is scaled from the hand-designed op-amp by two declared rules: compensation tracks the load, bias current tracks the power budget. Seeds are launch points, never results; across four spec profiles, two were met by the seed alone and the others within two iterations.
  • Optimizing from the form's current values remains available as the second path.
0.6.0 2026-08-24
  • The design iterator. Set targets for gain, bandwidth, phase margin and power, press Start, and a coordinate search sizes the circuit until every target is met. Each candidate is a full ngspice simulation; the measurements decide the next move, and nothing is ever predicted from a formula.
  • Verified end to end on the SKY130 op-amp: from a failing start at 28 degrees of phase margin, the search reached 73.4 dB gain, 6.8 MHz unity-gain bandwidth, 63 degrees of margin and 79 uW in nine simulations, and stopped the moment the spec was met.
  • The search runs as a server job. The page polls progress, shows the best point so far with a met or short verdict per target, and Stop actually stops it. Apply result loads the winning values and runs one confirming simulation, so what you see is measured, not remembered.
  • Candidates the simulator refuses, a bias that will not settle or a loop with no crossover, are recorded and skipped rather than ending the search.
0.5.0 2026-08-24
  • Circuits can now declare a design surface: which parameters an optimizer may move, and which measured numbers count as goals, each with a direction and a default target. The op-amp tunes its bias current, input pair, output driver and compensation network. The two-pole macromodel tunes its gain-bandwidth and second pole, and doubles as the fast test bed for the search itself.
  • The parameterized netlist the roadmap called for: every device size, current and capacitor in the op-amp is a form field with declared bounds, and the netlist is rebuilt from them on every run.
0.4.0 2026-08-24
  • A hand-designed two-stage Miller op-amp on SKY130: eight transistors, a mirror-loaded input pair, a common-source second stage, and compensation with a zero-nulling resistor. The shipped sizing measures 71.5 dB of open-loop gain, 13.6 MHz unity-gain bandwidth, 74.6 degrees of phase margin and 156 uW.
  • Measured open loop with the classic DC servo: an enormous inductor closes the loop at DC so the operating point is a stable unity buffer, and the AC drive arrives through an enormous capacitor whose zero-ohm source holds the input node, so the loop is open by impedance at every swept frequency.
  • One sweep yields the whole datasheet line: open-loop gain, unity-gain bandwidth, phase margin, power, and the servoed output voltage.
  • Removing the nulling resistor costs twenty degrees of phase margin on the measured device. That is the right-half-plane zero doing exactly what the textbooks say it does, and there is a preset for it.
  • A sizing that does not bias, with the output stuck at a rail, is refused with a message naming the measured voltage and which widths to rebalance.
0.3.0 2026-08-24
  • Phase margin, measured from a real loop gain sweep. Every measurement before this one came from a closed loop response, and phase margin is not in there: it is a property of the loop gain, so the loop has to be opened.
  • A two-pole op-amp macromodel to measure it on. A single-pole loop has a phase margin of about 90 degrees no matter what you do to it, so it cannot tell a correct measurement from one that always returns 90. This one has a phase margin that is a real closed form and moves over a wide range.
  • The netlist carries two electrically separate copies of the circuit, one closed and one with the loop cut at the amplifier input. They share only ground, so one sweep measures both and the two responses cannot disagree about where the samples fell.
  • Measured against the closed form: phase margin agrees to 0.001 degrees, crossover to 0.05 percent, loop gain to 0.03 dB.
  • Gain margin is deliberately not reported. A two-pole loop never reaches 180 degrees of phase shift, so its gain margin is infinite and any number there would be an artifact of where the sweep stopped.
  • A user manual at /manual, covering how to run a simulation, how to read the result, what every warning means, and what to do when something goes wrong. Its tests check it against the code it documents, so it fails if a circuit is added without being written up.
  • Parameter combinations that cannot be swept now return 400 with a message saying which way to move, instead of 500.

0.2.0 2026-08-23
  • SKY130 NFET common-source amplifier: the first circuit built on a real process design kit rather than on ideal components.
  • PDK resolution from the PDK_ROOT environment variable, falling back to C:\pdk, with a message that says what to install when the model library is missing.
